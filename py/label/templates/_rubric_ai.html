AI-product rubric

Mark AI product if generative AI — LLMs, diffusion / image / video generation, voice synthesis, generative agents — is a core part of the product's value proposition, as judged by what the release emphasises, and the product is sold into an end market rather than to the AI industry itself.

Tiebreakers, in order: (1) Who buys it? If the customer is someone building, training or operating generative AI systems → AI tooling. (2) Otherwise, take the generative model away — is it still substantially the same product? If no → AI product. If it merely loses a feature → AI feature. If the release only name-drops AI in boilerplate, a CEO quote or the company blurb → not AI.

Not AI (even though they sound like it): predictive analytics, forecasting, recommendation engines, fraud / risk scoring, computer-vision classification & detection, OCR, standalone speech recognition, robotic process automation, and any "AI-powered" claim with no mechanism named. Classic ML is not generative AI.

AI feature: not an AI product (still trained as a negative), but the release concerns a largely non-AI product that introduces a generative-AI feature — an assistant bolted onto an existing suite, an LLM summariser added to a CRM. The tiebreaker fails (take the model away and the product is intact) yet the release is not AI-free either. Its own class, ai_class: 2 — not a flavour of "no": these are the most confusable cases in the task, full of generative vocabulary, and the model is asked to tell them apart rather than to lump them in with plain negatives.

AI tooling: a real product release — not a skip — but one aimed at the AI industry and AI developers themselves: vector / RAG databases, agent and orchestration frameworks, fine-tuning and training platforms, inference and model-serving platforms, GPU clouds and compute providers, AI accelerators, training data and annotation services, eval, observability and guardrail tooling. The value proposition is generative AI, but what is sold is an input to somebody else's AI system rather than a generative product in its own right. Its own class, ai_class: 3, so the picks-and-shovels layer can be counted separately from AI products reaching an end market instead of being folded into either.

No product / N/A: the release isn't about a product at all (funding, earnings, hires, events, pure research). The question presupposes a product, so these are recorded as a reasoned skip rather than a negative label — they'd otherwise teach the model that "no product" means "not AI".

Hard calls: a genAI feature bolted onto an established non-AI product → AI feature, unless the release makes the generative capability the headline and the product's reason to exist → AI product. A product with no generative element at all is a plain not AI, not an AI feature. A foundation model or generative API sold to developers is still an AI product — the thing being sold generates — whereas the substrate it runs on or the framework that calls it is AI tooling; developers being the buyers is not by itself enough. Infrastructure sold to everyone, with AI workloads as one named use among many → judge by what the release leads on. A consultancy or services offering about AI → a defined product for AI builders is tooling, a defined product for an end market is AI, no defined product at all is a skip. Chatbots → judge the underlying mechanism; a scripted decision-tree bot is not AI. When genuinely unsure → skip.
