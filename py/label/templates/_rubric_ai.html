AI-product rubric

Mark AI product if generative AI — LLMs, diffusion / image / video generation, voice synthesis, generative agents — is a core part of the product's value proposition, as judged by what the release emphasises.

Tiebreaker: take the generative model away — is it still substantially the same product? If no → AI product. If it merely loses a feature → AI feature. If the release only name-drops AI in boilerplate, a CEO quote or the company blurb → not AI.

Not AI (even though they sound like it): predictive analytics, forecasting, recommendation engines, fraud / risk scoring, computer-vision classification & detection, OCR, standalone speech recognition, robotic process automation, and any "AI-powered" claim with no mechanism named. Classic ML is not generative AI.

AI feature: not an AI product (still trained as a negative), but the release concerns a largely non-AI product that introduces a generative-AI feature — an assistant bolted onto an existing suite, an LLM summariser added to a CRM. The tiebreaker fails (take the model away and the product is intact) yet the release is not AI-free either. Flagged via has_ai_feature so these can be reused later, and kept in training as hard negatives to curb false positives.

Counts as AI: infrastructure and tooling whose stated purpose is building, training or serving generative models — accelerators, inference platforms, vector stores, eval tooling. The value proposition is generative AI even though the product itself isn't a model.

No product / N/A: the release isn't about a product at all (funding, earnings, hires, events, pure research). The question presupposes a product, so these are recorded as a reasoned skip rather than a negative label — they'd otherwise teach the model that "no product" means "not AI".

Hard calls: a genAI feature bolted onto an established non-AI product → AI feature, unless the release makes the generative capability the headline and the product's reason to exist → AI product. A product with no generative element at all is a plain not AI, not an AI feature. A consultancy or services offering about AI → AI only if there's a defined product. Chatbots → judge the underlying mechanism; a scripted decision-tree bot is not AI. When genuinely unsure → skip.
