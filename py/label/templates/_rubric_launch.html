Relevance rubric

Mark relevant if the press release's primary purpose is to announce a specific product or service that is newly available from the issuing company.

Tiebreaker: after reading this, can I name a specific thing a customer could now buy, sign up for, or download that they couldn't before? If yes → relevant.

Relevant: new product launches; major releases with their own name; public beta / early access (if signups open); new services with defined scope; co-developed products.

Irrelevant: funding / M&A / earnings / hires; awards & analyst rankings; partnerships; regional expansion of existing products; rebrands; clinical/research milestones without a product; events; corporate updates; discontinuations.

Feature release: something new is shipping, but as an addition to an existing product rather than as a product of its own — a capability, module or integration inside something customers already have. Recorded as launch_class: 3, trained as a negative for now.

Feature or product? The line is genuinely blurred — use your judgement. A major release counts as a new product: its own name, its own pricing, buyable on its own, or a version customers have to move to. An incremental improvement to what they already have is a feature release: a capability added to an existing tier, a new integration, a performance or UI update, a patch. When the two pull in opposite directions, weigh how the release itself frames the thing.

Irrelevant w/ product info: not a launch and nothing new shipping either — but the release carries substantive detail about an already-launched product: capabilities, specs, pricing, customer wins. Recorded as launch_class: 2 so these can be reused later, and kept in training as hard negatives to curb false positives. Contrast with a feature release, where something new does ship.

Hard calls: "industry's first" hype doesn't count — apply the tiebreaker. Named & priced thing → new product. Update to a named existing product → feature release, or plain irrelevant if nothing actually ships. Free / open-source releases count if discrete. Security products live under domain software. When genuinely unsure → skip.
